How to calculate mesos per hour in MapleStory Classic

A simple way to compare maps with real numbers instead of vibes.

"This map makes good mesos" is one of the most common things said in MapleStory Classic and one of the least checked. Mesos per hour is the number that lets you compare two maps fairly, and it's easier to measure than most people think. Here's what to count, what to ignore, and where the numbers usually mislead you.

The basic formula

Mesos per hour is just total mesos gained over a stretch of farming, scaled to an hour:

  • Note your mesos at the start of a session.
  • Farm for a set time - at least 20 to 30 minutes, so one lucky or unlucky drop doesn't decide the result.
  • Note your mesos at the end, add the value of anything you're keeping to sell, and scale to one hour.

For example (made-up numbers, just to show the math): if you gain 300,000 mesos in 30 minutes, that's roughly 600,000 mesos/hr. Nothing fancy. The hard part is being honest about what goes into that total.

What to count

  • Direct meso drops. The mesos monsters drop on the ground. Easy to see, easy to count.
  • Items you'll vendor. Gear and misc drops that sell to NPCs are real income even though they don't show up in your meso count until you sell them. Skip this and a gear-heavy map looks worse than it is.
  • Items you'll trade. Useful, but treat prices as soft. Market prices move, so a rare drop's value on paper isn't the same as mesos in hand.
  • Costs. Potions, ammo and travel are real expenses. A map that earns well but burns through supplies can net less than a quieter one.

Why a single session lies to you

Drops are random. One good rare drop can make a mediocre map look amazing for an hour, and a dry streak can make a solid one look bad. A few habits help:

  • Run longer samples, and repeat a map on another day before you trust a result.
  • Count the same things the same way for every map, or the comparison means nothing.
  • Separate your steady income (mesos and common drops) from one-off jackpots when you decide where to farm.
  • Watch for crowding. The same map can pay noticeably differently depending on how many other players share it.

Mesos per hour isn't the whole picture

The best mesos map isn't always the best map for you. If a spot pays well but your character levels slowly there, you may be trading EXP for mesos without meaning to. Compare EXP per hour alongside mesos per hour, and decide which one you actually need right now. Early on, levels usually matter more. Later, mesos can.
