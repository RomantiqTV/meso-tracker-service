How to make mesos in MapleStory Classic: what actually adds up

Where mesos actually come from, and how to stop guessing at your hourly rate.

Everyone wants a quick answer to "how do I make mesos," and the honest one is boring: there isn't a single trick. Your mesos per hour comes from a handful of ordinary things stacking up, and most players only ever look at one of them. Here's how the pieces fit together.

Two sources: meso drops and item drops

Monsters pay you in two ways. The first is mesos that drop directly - you pick them up and your total goes up. The second is items, which only turn into mesos when you sell them to an NPC or to another player.

  • Direct meso drops are steady and predictable. They tend to grow with monster level, which is one reason moving up to a tougher map often raises your mesos per hour even if your kill speed stays flat.
  • Item drops are lumpier. Most of what drops is vendor trash worth very little; every so often something worth real money shows up. Over a long session the junk adds up more than you'd expect, but the occasional good drop is what swings a session from average to great.

Map choice matters more than grinding longer

Time spent is the least interesting variable. Two players farming for the same two hours can end up far apart just because of where they stood. What separates a good meso map from a bad one:

  • Monster density and respawn. Kills per minute is the multiplier on everything else. A tight spawn you can clear in a loop beats a high-level map where you spend half your time walking.
  • Drop table. Some monsters carry sellable gear or use-items that other players want. Others drop almost nothing of value. Check what a map's monsters can actually drop before settling in.
  • Your kill speed. A higher-level monster with a better table is only better if you can kill it without constantly drinking potions or dying. Potion spend comes straight out of your profit.

What to sell and what to hold

A few habits that quietly add up:

  • Don't vendor everything on autopilot. Some drops sell for more to other players than to an NPC. It's worth a quick look at what a given item goes for before you dump a full inventory.
  • Watch your inventory slots. A full inventory means you stop picking up drops, which means you're farming for free. Clear out junk on a schedule instead of when you notice.
  • Account for what you spend. Potions, travel and rebuying gear all reduce your real income. "Mesos gained" and "mesos earned" aren't the same number.

Measure, don't feel it out

The tricky part is that a good meso session and a mediocre one feel almost identical in the moment. You're killing things, numbers are going up, and the one big drop you remember skews your impression of the whole hour. The reliable way to compare two maps is to run each for a fixed stretch and compare the actual mesos per hour, not the highlights.
