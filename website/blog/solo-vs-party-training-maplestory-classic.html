Solo vs. party training: when does a party actually win?

The one condition that decides it, not a blanket "party is better" rule.

"Find a party, it's faster" is repeated so often it's treated as a given. It isn't - a party is a trade, not a bonus, and whether it pays off depends entirely on one property of the map you're standing on.

Why a party doesn't just add EXP

When your party kills a monster, that monster's EXP gets split across the party instead of going entirely to whoever landed the hit, and the party gets a bonus on top of the split. That bonus is real, but it has to overcome the split first - it isn't free EXP stacked on top of what you'd earn solo.

Community modeling puts a rough number on how big that gap is: six players at the same level need around four to five times the kill count of one solo player just to match that solo player's EXP/hr. That's the real cost of partying up, and it's a lot steeper than most players assume.

The one condition that decides it

A party only closes that gap - and comes out ahead - on a map with more monsters than one player could kill alone. If a solo player would spend half their time walking to the next spawn because there simply aren't enough monsters nearby, six players spread across that same map can be fighting constantly instead of walking. The party isn't winning because of the EXP bonus; it's winning because it eliminated the dead time a solo player can't avoid on an oversized or sparse map.

On a tight, dense map sized for one player - the kind that tops most solo EXP/hr rankings - there's no dead time to eliminate. Adding five more people just divides a fixed income five more ways. That's the entire rule, and it's a map property, not a character or class property:

  • Tight, single-player-sized map, high solo EXP/hr: stay solo. A party only dilutes it.
  • Sprawling map, more monsters than you can personally reach: a party converts your idle walking time into someone else's kill time. This is where partying genuinely pays.

Two cases where it's not just about the math

Even on a tight map where the EXP math says "stay solo," a party can still make sense for reasons the EXP/hr number doesn't capture:

  • Safety and sustain. A party shares aggro and can keep a weaker character alive on monsters they couldn't solo yet, trading some EXP efficiency for being able to train there at all.
  • It's genuinely more fun. If the alternative to a slightly-worse-EXP party is logging off because solo grinding feels tedious, the party wins by every metric that actually matters.

What the math argues against is the specific claim that a party is automatically faster. It isn't - it's situational, and the situation is written into the map, not into the word "party."
